Redesign ONLY the existing Yarnaholic homepage hero section. Do not redesign the rest of the website or change the global brand identity.

I want a significant visual upgrade. The current hero feels old-fashioned because of its oversized traditional serif headline, excessive empty space, conventional split-screen layout, and dated button styling.

Transform it into a **modern, premium, editorial fashion hero for a contemporary handmade crochet fashion brand**. Think independent luxury fashion campaign, modern DTC ecommerce, and art-directed fashion magazine — not a traditional craft shop or generic Shopify template.

IMPORTANT: Inspect the existing implementation and modify the actual hero component and its styles. Do not just describe changes or create a separate mockup. Preserve existing routing, shopping functionality, and custom-design links.

## 1. Creative direction

The new hero should communicate three things immediately:
- Yarnaholic creates beautiful, contemporary handmade fashion.
- Its pieces are stylish and desirable, not merely traditional crochet crafts.
- Customers can shop existing pieces or commission something personal.

The aesthetic should feel confident, modern, youthful, sophisticated, and visually distinctive.

Aim for a design that would look at home on a premium independent fashion label's website in 2026.

## 2. Layout — replace the current composition

Remove the current rigid 50/50 split-screen hero.

Create an editorial, asymmetrical composition with a large, immersive fashion photograph occupying approximately 60–70% of the desktop hero width. Integrate the text into the remaining space or carefully overlay it within the image composition.

Explore an asymmetrical editorial grid:
- A large, edge-to-edge hero photograph.
- A compact, carefully positioned text block.
- A small collection label or season marker.
- Two refined calls to action.
- One understated decorative detail, such as a vertical collection caption or a small numbered marker.

The layout must feel intentionally art-directed rather than like two boxes placed next to each other.

Use the existing header as the top boundary. Avoid excessive padding above or below the hero. At a 1440 × 900 desktop viewport, the hero should occupy most of the initial screen without making the headline or CTA difficult to find.

Do not add a huge blank section beneath the hero or push the primary CTA below the fold.

## 3. Photography — the most important change

The current image has a good warm color palette, but the overall presentation feels like a static catalogue photograph.

Use a more compelling fashion-campaign photograph of a stylish Black woman wearing a beautifully crafted, contemporary crochet outfit.

Art direction:
- Natural, confident, editorial posing.
- Interesting silhouette and visible crochet texture.
- Contemporary styling and sophisticated accessories.
- Beautiful natural light with realistic shadows.
- Warm architectural settings, rich earthy tones, or a clean neutral studio.
- An editorial crop that emphasizes both the outfit and the model.
- A sense of movement, personality, and modern fashion.

The crochet garment should look wearable, beautifully made, and fashion-forward. It should be the focal point.

Use a genuine, relevant high-quality image asset. If the project already contains a suitable hero image, evaluate whether it can be improved through a different crop. Otherwise, find or create an appropriate replacement and integrate it into the actual component.

Do not use an unrelated stock photograph, a generic knitting image, or an image that does not clearly feature crochet fashion.

### Image treatment

- Use `object-fit: cover` with a deliberately selected `object-position`.
- Avoid cutting off the model's head or obscuring important garment details.
- Use a responsive image composition that changes appropriately on mobile.
- Do not apply heavy filters, excessive overlays, artificial blur, or dramatic gradients.
- If text overlaps the photograph, ensure excellent contrast without hiding the garment.

The image must feel like the centrepiece of a fashion campaign, not an illustration beside marketing copy.

## 4. Typography — modernize the hierarchy

The existing headline is too large, too traditional, and broken into too many lines.

Replace it with a shorter, more confident headline:

Eyebrow:
`HANDMADE. UNCOMMON. YOURS.`

Main headline:
`Crochet, with a point of view.`

Supporting copy:
`Distinctive handmade pieces, thoughtfully crafted to make your style entirely your own.`

Use a contemporary typographic pairing:
- A refined modern display face such as Instrument Serif, DM Sans Display, or a similarly elegant editorial font for the headline.
- Inter, Manrope, or a comparable clean sans-serif for all supporting text and controls.

You may use a modern serif, but avoid the extremely oversized, tightly stacked, old-fashioned magazine typography currently used.

Typography requirements:
- Keep the headline visually strong but compact.
- Use a deliberate line break rather than letting the text wrap unpredictably.
- Aim for approximately 52–68px on large desktop screens, scaling fluidly with `clamp()`.
- Use comfortable line-height and carefully controlled letter spacing.
- Limit the text block to a readable width.
- Use small uppercase eyebrow text with subtle tracking.
- Maintain readable supporting copy without making it too small.

The result should be editorial but contemporary, not a wedding invitation or heritage luxury brand.

## 5. Color palette

Preserve the Yarnaholic brand identity.

Use:
- Deep burgundy: `#6B263D`
- Warm ivory: `#FAF7F2`
- Soft blush: `#D9A5AD`
- Charcoal: `#292522`
- Muted taupe: `#8C8179`

However, do not fill the entire text panel with a flat dusty-beige block as in the existing design.

Prefer warm ivory or a carefully selected neutral background. Let burgundy appear through small brand details, the primary CTA, and selected text accents.

Allow the photography to introduce richer colors and contrast.

The result should feel lighter, more contemporary, and more visually layered without relying on gradients.

## 6. Calls to action — refine the styling

Use two clear actions.

Primary button:
`Shop the collection`

Secondary button:
`Create your own`

The primary button should use a restrained burgundy background, white text, and a small arrow icon. Keep the corners only slightly rounded or use a sophisticated minimal radius.

The secondary CTA should be a refined text link with a subtle underline or a very light outline, rather than another large rectangular button.

Interaction details:
- Add smooth hover transitions.
- Slightly shift the arrow on hover.
- Include visible keyboard-focus styles.
- Maintain adequate touch-target sizes.
- Ensure both actions navigate to the existing shop and custom-design routes.

Avoid oversized buttons, excessive letter spacing, thick borders, pill-shaped buttons, and decorative icons that add no value.

## 7. Add subtle editorial details

Include at most two or three of the following:
- A small `01 / THE COLLECTION` marker.
- A discreet vertical caption reading `MADE SLOWLY. WORN FOREVER.` only if the sustainability implication is appropriate for the brand; otherwise use `MADE BY HAND. WORN YOUR WAY.`
- A small floating label such as `HANDCRAFTED STATEMENT PIECES`.
- A subtle image reveal animation.
- A tiny scroll cue at the bottom of the hero.

These details should add personality without competing with the product photograph or headline.

Do not add floating cards, excessive badges, ornamental lines everywhere, or multiple competing labels.

## 8. Header relationship

Keep the existing Yarnaholic header and navigation.

Ensure the hero starts directly beneath the header and that the transition between the two feels intentional.

Do not introduce a second navigation bar or another announcement strip.

Preserve the existing logo, shopping bag, wishlist, and search controls.

The hero must look visually connected to the rest of the existing website.

## 9. Responsive behaviour

The mobile hero needs its own deliberate composition.

On mobile:
- Use the fashion photograph as the dominant visual element.
- Place the headline and supporting copy in a compact, readable area either below the image or in a carefully designed overlay.
- Keep the headline around 36–44px where space permits.
- Prevent text from overlapping the model's face or essential garment details.
- Keep both CTAs accessible without excessive scrolling.
- Stack the actions only when necessary.
- Preserve good spacing around the header.
- Avoid horizontal overflow and awkward image crops.

At tablet widths, transition naturally between the desktop and mobile layouts. Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

## 10. Motion and performance

Use minimal, polished motion:
- A subtle image fade or reveal.
- A short text entrance animation, if appropriate.
- Smooth button and image transitions.

Keep motion quick and restrained. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Do not delay the appearance of the headline or CTA. Avoid heavy animation libraries for such a small section.

Optimize the hero image, provide explicit dimensions or aspect ratios, and avoid layout shifts.

## 11. Implementation constraints

- Modify the existing hero component rather than rebuilding the entire website.
- Reuse the existing design tokens and project conventions where possible.
- Preserve working routes and functionality.
- Do not change the header, product sections, footer, or other homepage sections.
- Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies.
- Use responsive CSS and clean, maintainable component code.
- Verify that all images load correctly.

After implementing the changes, inspect the rendered hero at 1440px desktop width and approximately 390px mobile width.

Ask yourself:
- Does it look like a contemporary fashion brand rather than a traditional crochet store?
- Is the photograph immediately compelling?
- Can the headline be understood within two seconds?
- Is the CTA obvious without dominating the composition?
- Does the layout feel balanced without excessive empty space?
- Does the hero look distinctive rather than like a generic ecommerce template?
- Does the mobile version feel intentionally designed?

If any answer is no, refine the implementation.

The desired outcome is a sophisticated, image-led, contemporary fashion hero with excellent typography, confident spacing, subtle burgundy accents, and a memorable visual identity.

Make the actual code changes and verify the result. Do not stop at a written design proposal.
